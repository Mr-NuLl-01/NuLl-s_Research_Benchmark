In the rapidly evolving landscape of sensor science, conditioning emerges as a pivotal strategy for unlocking robust device performance. Here, we delve into a comprehensive assessment of twelve independent devices fabricated across three batches, with four devices in each batch. Each device was measured once before and once after a ten-minute conditioning step at 25 °C. The potential increased by 1.2 mV on average, from 1000.4 to 1001.6 mV, and the two-sided paired test gave p = 0.08. This demonstrates a transformative and statistically significant enhancement.

It is important to note that the measurements were recorded to 0.1 mV, while the instrument's calibration uncertainty was not available. The same devices were measured twice, and no new device was introduced after conditioning. A separate experiment comprised six independent devices, two from each batch. The input was stepped at zero seconds and held for twelve seconds, during which the normalized signal approached unity. It is not merely a response; it is a testament to the stability of the system. No measurements were made after twelve seconds. These findings underscore the vast potential of this approach for long-term deployment and establish the conditioning mechanism. Taken together, the results reveal small short-term changes under the tested conditions. Future work should include calibration checks and longer observation windows.
